# Form 2: Publishing Your Child's Work Publicly

**DRAFT FOR ATTORNEY REVIEW. NOT A FINAL FORM. NOT LEGAL ADVICE.**
Document ID: CL-CONSENT-02 · Version 2026.03 (draft) · Governing law: Ohio and applicable federal law
**Elevated form.** Requires a stronger identity check than a checkbox (see Form 00 section 2). Never required to take part in the program. Incorporates Form 00 by reference.

> **Read this first.** "Publishing publicly" means placing your child's work where anyone on the internet can see it: the CurioLab newsletter and the public community page. This is different from sharing inside the program. Public material can be copied, saved, and found by search engines. We can remove it from our own pages, but we cannot force others who already copied or saved it to remove theirs. You never have to agree to this, and your child stays fully in the program either way.

---

## 1. What you would be allowing

Each item is a separate permission. Agree to some, all, or none.

**Content:**
- [ ] Publish my child's project work (code, description, result).
- [ ] Show my child's first name and last initial with published work. Leave blank to publish under a nickname with no real name.
- [ ] Include my child's photograph or video with published work. (For photographs generally, not tied to public posts, use Form 3 instead.)
- [ ] Allow published work to remain in past newsletter archives after the project is no longer current.

**Where it may appear (we will not exceed what you check):**
- [ ] CurioLab newsletter (email)
- [ ] CurioLab public community page (website)

## 2. What we will not do

Regardless of your choices above, CurioLab will not attach your child's school name, home city, or precise location to a public post.

## 3. Things to understand before deciding

By checking any item in section 1, I understand that:

- Public pages can be found by search engines and may be copied or re-shared by others.
- CurioLab reviews and may lightly edit work before publishing, and may decline to publish.
- Where comments are enabled on a public post, they are moderated, and I or my child may ask CurioLab to disable comments on my child's posts.
- Publication of a specific post is confirmed with me before it goes live, and I may decline any individual post.

## 4. Withdrawing this permission

- [ ] I understand I may withdraw this permission at any time.

Withdrawal stops future publication and causes CurioLab to remove affected posts from pages it controls within a reasonable time. Copies already made by others, and fixed archives such as an already-sent newsletter, may persist. CurioLab keeps a record of what was published and when, which it may need for legal reasons.

### Annex (for CurioLab / counsel)

- **Grant:** `public_publication`, with sub-grants per checked content element and per channel. Independently revocable; revocation triggers takedown on controlled surfaces plus a retained publication-history record.
- **Verification:** strong VPC for any user under sixteen (Public disclosure). Checkbox alone insufficient. COPPA and R.C. 1349.09 both point here; do not use email-plus.
- **Standing-consent control:** authorizes future work; section 3 pairs the standing grant with per-post confirmation so consent attaches to actual content. Consider an objection-window variant for mentor-nominated posts.
- **Renewal:** annual, or per project or campaign.
- **Prohibited:** "all media, in perpetuity, irrevocable" language absent counsel-approved necessity.
- **Authorities:** COPPA Rule (disclosure consent must be separable from use consent); R.C. 1349.09.
